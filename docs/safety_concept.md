# Safety concept

**This is a simulation. It produces no evidence of a performance level and none is claimed.** A
protective device is certified by its manufacturer through hardware, diagnostics and a development
process; nothing here substitutes for any of that. What this document does is state the reasoning
behind the protective geometry so that the geometry can be checked rather than trusted.

## What the protective function is, and what it deliberately is not

The protective stop trips on **any** return inside the protective field. It does not ask what the
return is. That is not a simplification, it is the defining property: a protective device must
respond to a person, a pallet, a dropped box and an unclassifiable smudge identically.

**It does not consume the people detector, and must not.** The classifier measures about 0.18
precision, while raw returns from a pedestrian are present in 100 percent of frames (V-11). Wiring
classification into a protective function would make safety depend on the weaker signal. A test in
`amr_safety` asserts the observation source is the merged scan and fails if any classifier topic
appears there.

The classifier's job is *behaviour*: yielding early, predicting a path, choosing a side. Those
degrade gracefully when it is wrong. The stop does not depend on it at all.

## Where the field geometry comes from

The field is the stopping distance, so it is calculated:

    S = v * (t_scanner + t_control + t_brake)   the vehicle keeps going while everything reacts
      + v^2 / (2 * a)                           braking
      + C                                       the scanner's own field supplement

This is the ISO 13855 shape of the calculation applied to a vehicle. `C` is the SICK sheet's 65 mm
protective field supplement, which exists because a scanner cannot resolve an object exactly at the
boundary.

| term | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| scanner response | 70 ms | SICK nanoScan3 sheet |
| control latency | 100 ms | **estimated, NOT measured. See open items.** |
| brake actuation | 50 ms | estimated |
| emergency deceleration | 1.5 m/s2 | **estimated, the weakest number here** |
| field supplement | 65 mm | SICK nanoScan3 sheet |

Two of those are estimates and they are called out rather than buried. The deceleration matters
most: the platform sheet publishes an acceleration *limit* of 0.3 m/s2, which is a comfort and
load-retention figure for normal driving and is emphatically not an emergency braking rate. Using it
would oversize every field by a factor of five and would be wrong in the direction that looks
conservative, which is its own kind of error.

Resulting fields, generated by `amr_safety/tools/generate_fields.py` and never hand-edited:

| speed band | protective reach | warning reach |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 to 0.30 m/s | 0.161 m | 0.491 m |
| 0.30 to 0.80 m/s | 0.454 m | 0.938 m |
| 0.80 to 1.40 m/s | 1.026 m | 1.766 m |
| 1.40 to 2.00 m/s | 1.838 m | 2.818 m |

Reaches are measured from the front face. Braking is quadratic in speed, which is exactly why a
single fixed field cannot serve a vehicle with this speed range: sized for 2 m/s it would reach
2.2 m from the centre and the robot could never enter an aisle.

**Rounding is outward only.** Emitting the polygon rounded to the nearest 0.1 mm made one field
0.454 m against 0.454 m required, and a field one micron short does not meet its own requirement.

## Verified behaviour

Measured on the running system with an obstacle 0.778 m ahead of the vehicle:

| command | active protective field | obstacle inside | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 m/s | 0.561 m from base_link | no | vehicle moves, at 0.081 m/s |
| 0.80 m/s | 0.854 m from base_link | yes | **vehicle stops, 0.000 m/s** |

Three things at once. The protective stop fires. The field switches with speed, so the same obstacle
is safe at one speed and not at another. And the 0.081 m/s at a 0.25 m/s command is the warning
field doing its job: the obstacle is inside the warning field, and the slowdown ratio is 0.3.

## Open items, and they are load bearing

- **Control latency is not measured.** It feeds the field size directly. An earlier version of the
  platform spec claimed it had been measured; it had not, and the claim was removed.
- **Emergency deceleration is an estimate.** Everything above scales with it.
- **A 4.25 degree seam remains in scanner coverage** (V-06) and the field geometry does not yet
  account for those two sectors being unobserved.
- **No e-stop, no manual reset, no localisation-lost interlock yet.** ISO 3691-4 requires a
  protective stop to be reset deliberately rather than to clear itself when the obstacle moves.
